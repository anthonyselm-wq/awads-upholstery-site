Convertible & Truck Top Work Built for Ohio Weather

A convertible top or truck bed top has one of the hardest jobs on any vehicle — it has to seal out weather, flex every time it opens and closes, and survive direct sun for years without cracking, yellowing, or leaking. In southwest Ohio, where summers bring intense UV exposure and winters bring hard freezes, that combination is especially rough on top material. Awad's Upholstery has fitted and replaced convertible tops and truck tops from our Amelia shop since 1960, and getting the fit right — not just the material — is what keeps a top from leaking or flapping at highway speed.

We work on cloth and vinyl convertible tops, rear window replacement, truck bed tops and tonneau-style covers, and the canvas and boot covers that go along with them. A top that's improperly fitted, even with brand-new material, will leak, whistle, or wear out fast — so we spend as much attention on the frame, rails, and tension as we do on the fabric itself.

Signs Your Top Needs to Be Replaced

Convertible and truck tops usually show clear warning signs before they fail completely:

  • A rear plastic window that's turned yellow, cloudy, or cracked, even if the top fabric still looks fine
  • Water leaking in along the seams or edges during rain
  • Fabric that's faded, chalky, or visibly worn thin from UV exposure
  • A top that flaps, whistles, or vibrates at highway speed
  • Seams splitting where the fabric flexes every time the top opens and closes
  • A truck bed top with torn fabric, broken bows, or a frame that no longer holds its shape
  • Mildew or staining on the underside of the top from trapped moisture

The rear window is often the first thing to go on a convertible top, especially on older vinyl-window tops, and it's common for us to replace just the window on an otherwise good top rather than the whole assembly.

Our Convertible & Truck Top Process

  1. Free estimate and inspection. We look at the top, frame, and rails to determine whether you need a full top replacement, a rear window replacement, or a repair to the existing top.
  2. Material selection. Choose between vinyl and cloth top material, and glass or plastic rear windows depending on your vehicle and budget.
  3. Frame and rail check. Before new material goes on, we make sure the frame, rails, and tension points are in good working order — a new top over a bad frame won't fit right.
  4. Cutting and sewing in-house. Top panels and windows are cut and sewn to fit your specific vehicle, not a generic universal pattern.
  5. Fitting and weather sealing. The top is fitted, tensioned, and checked for proper seal along all edges before it leaves the shop.

Vinyl vs. Cloth Convertible Tops

Vinyl tops are more affordable and hold up well to UV exposure, making them a common choice for daily-driven convertibles. Cloth tops — often a twill-weave material — have a more refined, factory-correct look on many vehicles and can offer better insulation against noise and temperature, but generally cost more and require a bit more care. We'll walk through which option fits your vehicle, your budget, and whether you're matching an original factory look or just want a top that holds up to daily use. Neither material is objectively better across the board — it really is a question of what matters most for how and where you drive the car.

Truck Bed Tops & Canvas Work

For truck owners, we fit and repair bed tops and camper-style canvas tops, replacing torn fabric, broken bows, and worn frame components so the top seals properly against weather again. This is common work for pickup owners who use their truck bed for storage or hauling gear that needs to stay dry, and for pontoon and camper-style boat tops that share a lot of the same canvas and frame principles as truck tops.

Why Weather Sealing Matters as Much as the Fabric

A top can be made from the best material available and still leak if it's not tensioned and sealed correctly against the frame and body. We check the weatherstripping and seal points as part of every top job, because a top that looks good but lets water in on the first rain isn't a job well done. This attention to fit is part of why tops we install tend to outlast the generic, poorly-fitted replacement tops sometimes sold online.

Boot Covers & Frame Adjustment

A convertible top boot — the cover that snaps over the folded top when it's down — often gets overlooked until it's cracked, mismatched, or missing entirely. We fit and replace boot covers alongside top replacements, and can usually match the color and material to your new top for a finished look. If the top frame itself has gotten loose, bent, or is no longer holding proper tension after years of use, we can also make frame adjustments as part of the job so the top opens, closes, and seals the way it's supposed to instead of fighting the mechanism every time.

Caring for Your Top Between Services

Keeping a convertible top clean with products made for the specific material — vinyl or cloth — rather than generic cleaners helps prevent the premature fading and stiffening that leads to cracking. Parking under cover when possible, and avoiding raising or lowering the top in freezing temperatures when vinyl and cloth are at their most brittle, both help extend the life of a top between replacements. For truck bed tops, keeping the frame and hardware free of rust and checking snaps or latches periodically prevents small issues from turning into torn fabric.

Common Questions

Convertible & Truck Top FAQ

How long does a convertible top typically last?

It depends heavily on material, sun exposure, and how often it's used, but most convertible tops in our climate hold up well for several years before UV damage and wear make replacement worth considering. A rear window often needs attention before the top fabric itself does.

Can you replace just the rear window instead of the whole top?

Yes, if the top fabric itself is still in good shape, replacing just the rear window is often the more economical option and something we do regularly.

Vinyl or cloth — which top material should I choose?

Vinyl is more budget-friendly and very UV resistant; cloth tops often look closer to factory-original and can offer better sound insulation but typically cost more. We'll help you weigh the trade-offs for your specific vehicle.

How long does a top replacement take?

Timelines vary with the scope of the job and the season — spring is our busiest stretch for convertible top work. We'll give you a specific estimate for your vehicle when you bring it in.

Do you offer any warranty on top installations?

We stand behind our workmanship on every top we install. Ask us about the specifics for your job when you come in for your estimate.

What causes a top to leak even after replacement?

Leaks are almost always a fit or tensioning issue rather than a material issue — if a top isn't seated and sealed correctly against the frame and body, water finds a way in no matter how good the fabric is. That's why we check weatherstripping and seal points on every job.

Do you fix truck bed tops, or only convertible tops?

Both. We repair and replace truck bed tops and camper-style canvas tops in addition to convertible tops, using the same in-house cutting, sewing, and fitting process.

Can you adjust a top frame that's no longer sealing properly?

In many cases, yes. A loose or misaligned frame is a common cause of leaks and wind noise, and we can often correct tension and alignment issues as part of a top replacement or repair.
